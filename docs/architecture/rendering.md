# Rendering architecture: rules

The rules rendering changes keep. The design and its reasons are in `docs/src/gpu-rendering.md`; the plan and its
checklist are in `docs/plans/gpu-rendering.md`. Each rule names the plan step from which it is in force; before that
step it is the target the step must reach, and no change may move the tree away from it. A change that needs to break
a rule changes this file first, in its own reviewed change.

## Who draws what

1. *(in force)* The compositor draws every material (glass, blur, backdrop) and every effect that needs what is
   behind a window. Clients never sample the screen and never shade glass; they describe shapes and styles through
   `cupertino_backdrop_v1`, and paint a fill themselves when the effects protocol is absent or the window is past its
   material limit.
2. *(from step 7)* A client draws its own content with the toolkit renderer of its surface class: CPU for small,
   rarely shown, must-draw and static surfaces (the lock screen and login always), GPU for large or continuously
   repainted windows. The class of every shell process and of third-party applications is listed in the design; each
   process declares its own (`RendererClass`) and no switch turns the GPU on for every process.
3. *(in force)* The CPU renderer is the standard the GPU renderer is tested against, and the fallback. It is never
   removed and never allowed to fall behind the GPU renderer in what it can draw.

## One definition of the maths

4. *(from step 1)* Coverage of rectangles, rounded rectangles, strokes and clips; the Gaussian kernel with its
   pyramid, edges, alpha and precision; shadows; gradient interpolation; and dithering have one definition, as plain
   Rust with test vectors in the graphics layer. Every renderer and every shader implements that definition and is
   tested against its vectors.
5. *(from step 1)* Dithering is keyed to the pixel position. Nothing that reaches a golden is random.
6. *(from step 1)* After the look freeze, goldens are re-recorded only by a change that names the pixels it changes
   and shows a before/after sheet. *(from step 6)* Both toolkit renderers agree within the golden tolerance (2/255 per
   channel).
7. *(from step 1)* Glyph and path coverage come from our CPU rasterizers on every path; a GPU only places and tints
   them, glyphs at quarter-pixel x phase and whole-pixel y.
8. *(from step 1)* A drawn scroll offset is a whole number of device pixels. *(from step 4)* Retained layers exist in
   both toolkit renderers.

## Shaders

9. *(from step 5 for new programs, from step 10 for all)* Our own programs have one source in WGSL; their GLSL ES
   3.00 and SPIR-V are generated by the build step and committed, and a test fails when the committed output differs
   from a fresh generation.
10. *(from step 10)* The only hand-written shaders are the texture programs compiled through Smithay's shader
    interface (GLSL ES 1.00, with the external-sampler variant), each listed by name in the design.
11. *(in force)* The glass maths keeps its CPU reference model in `cupertino-glass`, and the shader is tested against
    it.

## Buffers and synchronisation

12. *(from step 6)* Toolkit clients speak Wayland in Rust. No toolkit client hands EGL or Vulkan a `wl_display`; GPU
    clients allocate their own buffers on the device named by dmabuf feedback and attach them through
    `zwp_linux_dmabuf_v1`.
13. *(from step 6)* A buffer uses a modifier the feedback offers and the client's driver can render to. Linear is
    never added on a hardware driver.
14. *(from step 6)* Synchronisation is explicit: acquire and release points through `linux-drm-syncobj-v1`, or,
    without that protocol, a client-side wait on its own fence before commit. Nothing relies on implicit
    synchronisation.
15. *(from step 6)* A surface that cannot use dmabuf (failed import, failed allocation) falls back to `wl_shm` alone;
    the syncobj surface object is destroyed before a shared-memory attach.

## Failure and fallback

16. *(from step 6)* EGL, GBM and Vulkan are loaded at run time; their absence selects the CPU renderer, never a failed
    start.
17. *(from step 6)* A process falls back to the CPU on `CUPERTINO_RENDERER=cpu`, missing dmabuf feedback, a render node
    it cannot see, an unusable or software device, or three context losses within a minute. Video-memory purges are
    never counted as losses. `CUPERTINO_RENDERER=gpu` forces the GPU path for tests. *(from step 7)* A fallback of a
    process or surface that wanted the GPU is logged and recorded as a diagnostic event.
18. *(from step 6)* Every client context is created with robustness and purge notification. After a reset or purge
    the compositor tells clients their buffers were lost, and clients repaint.
19. *(from step 6)* The renderer each process uses is logged, reported by `cupertino-diagnose` and recorded in the
    frame statistics.

## Structure

20. *(from step 1)* The shared maths, and from step 5 `cupertino-gpu`, live in the graphics layer, which the toolkit,
    the render layer and the compositor may depend on; it depends on the foundation layer only. *(in force)* Smithay
    stays in `cupertino-server` and `cupertino-seat`, and the toolkit never depends on the render layer.
21. *(from step 5)* The compositor runs `cupertino-gpu` passes inside Smithay's context only through the
    borrowed-context mode and its state-restore contract.
22. *(in force)* A new rendering protocol, or a new version of one, has an entry in
    `docs/architecture/contracts.yaml`. Geometry that must land on device pixels at fractional scales is carried in
    fixed point.

## Budgets

23. *(from step 0)* No change makes a bench scenario miss refreshes it met before, or raises its p99 above its
    baseline by more than the run-to-run noise recorded with the baseline; the look freeze alone may cost up to its
    10% budget.
    *(from the step that gates it)* Each scenario fits in one refresh of the target display. The budgets in the design
    are gates: a change that misses one is fixed or reverted, not shipped.
24. *(from step 7)* An idle desktop draws no frames, does no GPU work, and wakes no process except by the timers listed
    in the plan's measurements.
